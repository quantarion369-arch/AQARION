# Scalar temporal circuits and transition rank capacity

## Status and conventions

Written mathematical derivations for review, accompanied by targeted
exact checks. Not a machine-checked proof, independent referee report,
novelty clearance, or submission.

Work over Q. X is a finite nonempty set, N=|X|, T:X->X, and h:X->Q.
Put Uf=f composed with T and

    W = span_Q{1,h,Uh,U^2h,...}.

An observable system has distinct infinite scalar output histories.
An affine circuit has row rank N-1 and a one-dimensional row-relation
space generated by a vector with every coefficient nonzero.

Scalar labels and categorical one-hot encoding are different models.

## 1. Nonbijective scalar circuit classification

Assume observability, affine-circuit minimality, and nonbijective T.
Let v generate the full-support relation and k=|T(X)|.

Since W is U-invariant, its annihilator is invariant under push-forward:

    (T_*v)(y) = sum_{x:T(x)=y} v_x,
    T_*v = lambda v.

At a state outside T(X), full support forces lambda=0.
Every nonempty fiber has coefficient sum zero and therefore contains
at least two states. Thus N>=2k.

Define the future space INCLUDING the constant:

    L = span_Q{1,Uh,U^2h,...}.

Every member of L is constant on transition fibers, so dim L<=k.
Since W=L+Qh,

    N-1 = dim W <= k+1.

Consequently 2k<=N<=k+2, hence k<=2 and N<=4.

### Exact cases

N=1 cannot be a circuit because the constant row is nonzero.
N=2 is excluded by observability: two distinct unital rows are independent.

N=3:
T must be constant. Observability is equivalent to three distinct current
outputs. Those three rows have rank two and every pair is independent.
These conditions are necessary and sufficient.

N=4:
There must be two fibers F0={x1,x2}, F1={x3,x4}, each of size two.
Necessary and sufficient conditions are:

- h(x1) differs from h(x2);
- h(x3) differs from h(x4);
- h differs on the two distinct image states y0 and y1.

Within-fiber pairs are distinguished at time zero; cross-fiber pairs
are distinguished at time one.

The columns 1 and Uh are independent because h(y0)!=h(y1).
They span the two-dimensional fiber-constant space.
The column h lies outside it. Thus rank W=3.

Write D0=h(x1)-h(x2), D1=h(x3)-h(x4). A relation is

    v=(D1,-D1,-D0,D0)

in the stated fiber order. It annihilates 1 and h, and cancels within
each fiber for all future columns. Both D0,D1 are nonzero.
Rank three and the full-support relation imply every proper row subset
is independent.

For necessity of the image-output condition, note that T(X) is forward
invariant. If its two states have equal observations, their complete
histories coincide.

No observable nonbijective scalar temporal circuit exists at any other N.

For N>=3, circuit minimality itself excludes duplicate rows.
Observability is nevertheless retained explicitly to exclude the
two-identical-row N=2 case.

## 2. Multichannel necessary bound

Let H0 contain 1, let dim H0=r0, and let

    W = span{U^t f : f in H0, t>=0}.

Assume rank N-1, full-support unique relation, and nonbijective T.
The same push-forward argument gives N>=2k.

The positive-time space L has dimension at most k and contains 1.
Thus dim(H0 intersection L)>=1 and

    N-1 = dim W <= r0+k-1.

Hence N<=r0+k<=r0+N/2, giving N<=2r0.

This is necessary, not sufficient.
Scalar nonconstant h gives r0=2.
One-hot output using q labels gives r0=q.
The scalar N<=4 theorem must not be applied unchanged to categorical data.

## 3. Output recoding

Invertible affine recoding h'=a*h+b, a!=0, preserves the temporal space.

Arbitrary permutations of scalar values need not preserve rank.
For T=(1,2,3,0):

    h=(0,1,3,2) has rank 3;
    h=(0,1,2,3) has rank 4.

These observations differ by swapping two scalar labels.
Categorical label permutations instead permute one-hot columns.

An available alphabet need not have all its labels used.

## 4. Maximum scalar rank for a fixed transition

Let cycle lengths be l1,...,lc, and let nu be maximum distance to a cycle.
Define

    D = degree lcm_i (x^li-1).

Equivalently, D counts the distinct complex roots in the union of
the cycle root sets. Over Q,

    max_h dim W(h) = nu + D + indicator(c>=2).

"Generic" here means a nonempty Zariski-open set of rational observations,
not every injective observation and not necessarily a binary observation.

### Proof

The minimal polynomial of U is

    x^nu * lcm_i(x^li-1).

Transient blocks contribute the largest zero-eigenvalue Jordan block,
of size nu. Nonzero cycle eigenvalues are semisimple in characteristic
zero. Their minimal-polynomial contribution is the stated lcm.

Quotient by the invariant line Q*1. Then

    dim W(h) = 1 + dimension of the cyclic span of h modulo Q*1.

The maximal cyclic-span dimension equals the degree of the quotient
operator's minimal polynomial.

If c=1, quotienting removes the entire eigenvalue-1 space and decreases
that degree by one.
If c>=2, a nonzero eigenvalue-1 space remains, so the factor stays.

Maximal cyclic vectors form a nonempty algebraic open set.
All other factors retain their maximal block sizes under this quotient.
This gives the formula.

This proof uses standard cyclic-subspace/minimal-polynomial machinery.
Literature priority for this application has not been established.

## 5. Characterization of full-rank-capable maps

Let t=N-sum_i li be the number of transient states.
We have nu<=t and D<=sum_i li-(c-1).

For c>=3, maximal rank is at most N-c+2<N.

For c=1, full rank is possible exactly when nu=t.
For c=2, use D=l1+l2-gcd(l1,l2). Full rank is possible exactly when

    nu=t and gcd(l1,l2)=1.

The condition nu=t says all transient states form one chain entering a
cycle. With no transient states, this condition is vacuous.

This characterizes existence of a suitable rational scalar observation.
It is not a statement that all observations are full rank.

## 6. Counting the labeled full-rank-capable maps

Let C_m count permutations on m labeled points with either one cycle
or two cycles of coprime lengths. Then

    C_m = (m-1)! +
          (m!/2) sum_{1<=a<m, gcd(a,m-a)=1} 1/[a(m-a)].

The half avoids ordering the two cycles.

For N states, choose m cyclic labels, their permutation, the order of
the N-m transient states, and (when nonempty) the chain attachment
among the m cyclic vertices:

    A_N = C_N +
          sum_{m=1}^{N-1} binom(N,m)*(N-m)!*m*C_m.

An equivalent expression uses
S_m=sum_{1<=a<m,gcd(a,m)=1}1/a, with S_1=0:

    C_m=(m-1)!*(1+S_m),

    A_N=N!*sum_{m=1}^{N-1}(1+S_m)+(N-1)!*(1+S_N).

Values for N=1,...,10:

    1,4,23,146,1014,8124,68592,681456,7112592,82444320.

The checker validates formula arithmetic against these pinned targets.
It does not independently enumerate maps. The combinatorial derivation,
not numerical agreement, supplies the general counting argument.

## 7. Computational and research boundaries

verify.py checks nine explicit positive/negative examples using Fraction
elimination, N scalar observation columns, row-deletion ranks, and
specified relation vectors. It also checks ten count-formula values.

It does not rerun any original exhaustive census, authenticate another
assistant's historical execution, verify Case-B crossing bounds, perform
a literature-priority audit, or machine-check the general proofs.

No PB theorem is reopened. No manuscript or old archive is rewritten.
Source code and execution artifacts belong to this new package only.
