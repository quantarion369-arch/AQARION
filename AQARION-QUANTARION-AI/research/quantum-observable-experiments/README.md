# Quantum observable experiments

Exact two-level model experiments using Python standard-library rational
arithmetic. This is a computational research collection, not a neutrino
simulation, experimental tomography result, or Lean-certified theorem.

## Conventions

H = (h_x X + h_y Y + h_z Z)/2, with hbar = 1.
The Heisenberg generator is L*(O) = i[H,O].
On Pauli coefficients, K(a) = a cross h.
Dimensions include the constant observable I.

## General generator argument

Starting from e_z, the spin observable space is generated by
e_z, K e_z, and K^2 e_z.

det[e_z, K e_z, K^2 e_z] = -h_z(h_x^2+h_y^2).
K^3 = -||h||^2 K.

A parallel axis gives dimension 2 including I.
A nonzero perpendicular axis gives dimension 3.
An axis with nonzero longitudinal and transverse components gives dimension 4.

The identities and the exceptional cases supply the mathematical argument.
The 125-point regression checks an implementation, not all real parameters.

## General uniform-sampling argument

For a nonzero Hamiltonian let u = h/||h||, theta = ||h|| Delta,
c = cos(theta), and s = sin(theta).

R(a) = c a + (1-c)u(u dot a) + s(a cross u).

det[e_z, R e_z, R^2 e_z] = -2 u_z(1-u_z^2)s(1-c).
R^3 - (1+2c)R^2 + (1+2c)R - I = 0.

Parallel axes or full-turn sampling retain only one spin direction.
A perpendicular axis at a half-turn also retains one spin direction.
A perpendicular axis at a nonexceptional angle retains two.
A tilted axis at a half-turn retains two.
A tilted axis with sin(theta) nonzero retains three.
Add one for the constant observable.

The zero Hamiltonian gives dimension 2 including I.
The finite regression checks 156 exact axis-rotation pairs.

## Evidence boundaries

- Single-time expectations use separately prepared copies.
- These are not repeated invasive measurements on one evolving system.
- Finite-dimensional observable closure is not a finite-state model.
- Linear closure does not imply closure under operator multiplication.
- A smaller linear predictor is not automatically a smaller quantum subsystem.
- Saved logs and exit codes are local execution records.
- Source hashes identify bytes; they do not establish mathematical correctness.
- General scope labels in logs are not formal proof certificates.
- No Lean certification, experimental validation, or novelty claim is made.
- Original source hashes and execution records are preserved unchanged.
- Environment records differ in detail; missing historical details are not invented.
